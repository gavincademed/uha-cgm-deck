THE DME REQUIREMENT

DME has to follow up. Pharmacy doesn't.

Every ninety days, DME has to confirm the patient is still using the device before sending the next supply. That check is why adherence holds.

The DME 90-day resupply cycle
Every 90 daysConfirm continued use before resupply ships
DocumentationMedical necessity, insulin, hypoglycemia history
Visit cadenceRoutine in-person or telehealth follow-up
Adherence checkUtilization reviewed; outreach if it drops
ReplacementFailed sensors caught and replaced fast
This is the federal standard. Medicare covers CGM as DME and requires consistent device use and routine visits.
PHARMACY MODEL

Fill the script, hand it over. No 90-day check. No follow-up if the patient stops. A sensor dispensed but never worn still bills.

Dropping off CGM costs more later. When patients fall off therapy, diabetes goes unmanaged and comorbidities pick up. The payor pays for it down the road.
CADE does this work. We carry the follow-up, the documentation, and the resupply checks. UHA gets one adherence story and one reporting source.
Source: CMS, Therapeutic CGM coverage · ADA, Medicare CGM coverage criteria.

THE PHARMACY HEADACHE

Local pharmacies lose money on CGM.

PBMs pay them less than it costs to buy and dispense the device. The pharmacy still has to do the follow-up. That is why they are walking away.

What the PBM does to the independent
Below-cost payReimbursed under acquisition cost on some fills
Spread pricingPBM keeps the gap between what UHA pays and the pharmacy gets
DIR clawbacksFees pulled back months after the fill
Follow-up burdenPharmacy still chases resupply and documentation, unpaid
They lose twice. They lose money on the dispense, and they spend staff time on follow-up they aren't paid for. CGM is one of the first product lines independents drop.
THE PATTERN

Over 3,250 net pharmacy closures in 2024 alone, independents hit hardest. CGM is exactly the kind of high-cost, low-margin line they cut first.

CADE is the relief valve. Pharmacies leave CGM because it loses money and eats their time. We take on the dispensing, documentation, and follow-up. We do not compete with them. We take the part that is hurting them.
